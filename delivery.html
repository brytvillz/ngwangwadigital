The Problem — Validated

Vendors in Nigeria Are Losing Customers to Logistics Chaos

I did not guess this. I sat with vendors and asked them directly. Five of them told me the same five things — in different words, but the same pain.

💸

Unpredictable Pricing

"Riders quote different prices every single time. I can never plan."

👻

Riders Disappear

"They say they are coming, then they switch off. Customer is waiting."

👁️

No Visibility

"I never know where my rider is or if the delivery happened."

🕐

Time Wasted Daily

"I spend two hours every day just haggling and chasing riders."

😤

Lost Sales

"Customers stopped ordering because delivery is too stressful."

"Lost sales because delivery was too stressful." — Vendor, Enugu Tech Fest, January 2026. All five vendors confirmed the same pain points before a single line of code was written.

Chapter 01

3MTT Impact Summit — Where It Started

I am a 3MTT Fellow. I completed the AI/ML learning track — all fundamental courses, all certifications. At the Impact Summit Hackathon, standing in that room with builders, mentors, and people who were actually shipping things, the question hit me differently.

Not "can I build something?" — I already knew I could. The question was: can I build something that solves a pain people feel every single day?

Logistics in Nigeria. Moving goods. The chaos I had watched vendors deal with. That was the problem. I left the summit and started planning NgwaNgwa Delivery.

Chapter 02

15 Days. One Accident. Zero Stops.

I gave myself 15 days to build the MVP. On day 5, I had an accident. My hand was bandaged. This photo was taken that same day — the NgwaNgwa Delivery app on screen, wrapped hand on the keyboard.

Nobody told me to keep going. I just did not have a reason to stop. The app needed to exist. Vendors needed a booking flow, a tracking system, a rider panel, an admin panel. I built all of it from my bed.

Shipped in 15 days. Built with vanilla HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. No framework. No team. No funding. Just the work.

Chapter 03

On Stage at Enugu Tech Fest

I showed up at the Enugu Tech Fest with what I had built and asked for a chance to present. I got it. Standing on that stage with a microphone and a working product I had built from scratch — that moment changed something.

Five vendors signed up at the event before the product launched. Not because of a pitch deck or a demo video — because they felt the problem I was describing. That was all the validation I needed.

The pilot launch date was set: March 3, 2026.

Chapter 04

Listening to Real People

Before writing another line of code, I went to vendors and riders — not to pitch, but to listen. I sat with a logistics manager and asked her to walk me through a typical week. I asked a rider to explain where trust breaks down.

Both sides had real pain. Vendors felt abandoned. Riders felt disrespected. The breakdowns happened in the middle — in the gap between a booking and a delivery, where there was no system, no transparency, and no accountability.

Every feature in NgwaNgwa Delivery came from those conversations. Not from assumptions. Not from a textbook. From the people who would actually use it.

🚚 Photo coming — the live pilot days
Chapter 05

3 Days. Real Operations.

The pilot launched on March 3, 2026. Riders came on board. Vendors placed real bookings through the platform. The admin panel tracked everything. The system worked.

Three days of live operations confirmed what the research had shown — the product solved a real problem. Vendors responded. Riders showed up. The platform held.

The product did not fail. The funding ran out. Without capital to pay riders, maintain operations, and sustain marketing, we had to pause. Zero sales — not because the idea was wrong, but because the timing was.

🌱 The pivot that kept everything alive
Chapter 06

The Pivot — Not the End

NgwaNgwa Delivery is not dead. It is paused with intention.

I started NgwaNgwa Digital — a web and digital agency — to generate the capital needed to fund the delivery operations properly. Every website we build, every client we serve, every product we ship moves us closer to the moment we relaunch this with the resources it deserves.

The pivot was not a retreat. It was a strategy. You cannot build a logistics company without logistics capital. So we are earning it, building our reputation, and staying ready.

When NgwaNgwa Delivery comes back, it will come back funded, validated, and with a team.
